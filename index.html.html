Builder / Auditor Protocol for AI-Assisted Engineering

Rule 0 — the ring itself

Truth and verification outrank confidence, elegance, speed and agreement.

A verified claim and an unverified claim MUST NOT be presented in a form that makes them indistinguishable. The tool advises; the human decides. Every control in this document exists to keep that distinction visible under time pressure.

1Scope and applicability

What this protocol governs, and what it deliberately leaves alone.

This specification applies to engineering and building work in which an AI system diagnoses, designs, modifies, tests or reviews software. It does not apply to conversational, exploratory or research use, and imposes no ceremony there.

The design objective is to raise the probability that AI-assisted engineering work is correct, bounded, reversible where possible, independently reviewable, and supported by evidence proportionate to its consequence.

ORF is a governance and audit overlay, not a software-development lifecycle. It is designed to sit on top of existing engineering methods, including spec-driven development, test-driven development and project-specific delivery processes. Where a governing project specification exists, ORF uses it as audit input rather than replacing it.

This specification is not

  • A requirement to run all eleven principles on every task.
  • A substitute for runtime tests, security testing, observability or human accountability.
  • A model-consensus mechanism. Agreement between models is not evidence.
  • Permission for an Auditor to rewrite a build without an explicit role change.
  • An adopted industry standard. It is a proposed open specification, offered for public review and criticism.

2Terms and definitionsNew in this revision

Three words carry most of the load in this protocol. Undefined, they are the easiest place for it to fail quietly.

ORF-2.1

Material. A claim, assumption or disagreement is material if the decision would change were it false. Immaterial items are not exempt from honesty, only from ceremony. When materiality is itself disputed, the item is treated as material.

ORF-2.2

Blast radius. The set of users, data, systems and commitments affected if the change behaves incorrectly — not the size of the diff.

ORF-2.3

Independent. Review conducted without the builder's session context, without the builder's reasoning, and without authorship attribution. See §8 for degrees of independence.

ORF-2.4

Decision owner. The named human accountable for the outcome. Roles may be delegated; accountability is not.

ORF-2.5

Governing specification. The project-specific source of requirements and constraints against which a change is built and audited. It may include a product or technical specification, acceptance criteria, constitution or construction file, ADRs, security requirements, interface contracts or equivalent project artifacts.

ORF-2.6

Change Record. The persistent evidence record for one engineering change. It accumulates classification, scope decisions, verification, audit findings, coverage and human decisions so the protocol does not regenerate the same context as separate artifacts.

3Normative architecture

The pipeline is a stateful loop, not a line. Each return path is explicit.

StageRequired control
RULE 0Truth and verification govern all downstream decisions.
CLASSIFYDeclare PATCH, CHANGE or COMMITMENT with rationale.
BUILDApply the controls the declared tier requires.
STOPSurface scope expansion before acting on it.
VERIFYUse evidence appropriate to the class of claim.
AUDITIndependent review where the tier requires it; provenance withheld by default.
COVERAGEAuditor states checked, not checked, and runtime required.
RESOLVETestable disputes to evidence; non-testable disputes to the human.
SIGN-OFFHuman approval where the tier requires it.
CLOSERecord final state, residual risk and unresolved items.
SAMPLEPeriodically audit tier choices and Verified claims.

Required state transitions Clarified in public review

TriggerRequired next state
New engineering taskCLASSIFY before substantive implementation.
Scope expansion discoveredSTOP → human decision. If approved, the expanded work returns to CLASSIFY. If rejected, remain inside prior scope or DEFER.
Verification failure requiring implementation changeReturn to CLASSIFY before remediation. A diagnostic re-run that changes no implementation may remain in VERIFY.
Audit finding requiring remediationReturn to CLASSIFY. The remediation does not inherit the tier of the original change.
Runtime-required claim unresolvedRemain UNRESOLVED until runtime evidence is obtained, or route the unresolved risk to the human decision owner.
Non-testable material disagreementLog an OPEN QUESTION and route to the human decision owner.
PATCH verification succeedsCLOSE, unless the task escalated or a Stop Rule decision is outstanding.
CHANGE / COMMITMENT verification succeedsAUDIT → COVERAGE → RESOLVE as required → SIGN-OFF where required → CLOSE.
ORF-3.1

A transition that changes implementation scope, behaviour or risk MUST pass through CLASSIFY before implementation continues.

ORF-3.2

A protocol state MUST NOT be treated as closed while a required Stop Rule decision, required audit, required sign-off or undisclosed material unresolved claim remains outstanding.

4Classification and the ratchet

Classification uses impact, reversibility and uncertainty together. The tier controls ceremony — governance must not itself become unnecessary engineering overhead.

TierTypical profileMandatory protocol
PATCH Low impact, readily reversible, low uncertainty. Rule 0, classification, Stop Rule, proportionate verification, footer. No mandatory independent audit.
CHANGE Meaningful impact or meaningful reversal cost or material uncertainty. Principles 1–8, verification, independent Auditor, Coverage Statement, footer.
COMMITMENT High impact, difficult or costly to reverse; security, privacy, financial, public or architectural consequence; or high uncertainty. All eleven, strongest appropriate runtime verification, independent Auditor, Coverage Statement, explicit human sign-off.

Classification requirements

ORF-4.1

Where a task spans tiers, the highest applicable tier MUST be used.

ORF-4.2

Uncertainty about blast radius is itself an escalation signal and MUST be treated as such.

ORF-4.3

The Builder MAY escalate PATCH → CHANGE → COMMITMENT at any point, without permission.

ORF-4.4

The Builder MUST NOT lower a declared tier, including when the work proves easier than expected.

ORF-4.5

Only the decision owner may downgrade a tier. The downgrade MUST be recorded with rationale.

ORF-4.6

A fix arising from an audit finding MUST NOT inherit the original tier. It re-enters at CLASSIFY (§11).

Rationale

Self-classification carries a downward incentive: a lower tier is less work. The ratchet removes the incentive by making escalation free and downgrade a recorded human act. The declared tier is also the cheapest control point available — PATCH — replacing the authentication model is a visible error in one line, without reading the diff.

5The eleven principles

Frozen at eleven. Applied according to tier, not uniformly.

  1. Decompose and budgetObjective, constraints, stakeholders, dependencies, success criteria and implementation budget. Without a declared budget, scope creep cannot be violated, only felt.
  2. Floor firstThe smallest viable change is the default. Anything larger must justify its complexity before it is built.
  3. ChallengeConstruct the strongest credible alternative and state the conditions under which it would win.
  4. CalibrateSeparate known, inferred, assumed and unknown material claims. No material assumption silently becomes fact.
  5. EvidenceVerify claims that materially affect the decision; label what was not checked, where it is stated.
  6. Decide with failure conditionRecommend when evidence permits, and state what would have to be true for the recommendation to fail.
  7. Failure-testHunt edge cases, partial failures, concurrency, hidden dependencies, security risks and irreversible steps.
  8. Stop ruleIf anything outside approved scope becomes necessary: stop, surface it, hand the decision back.
  9. Subtract, then synthesizeAfter success, simplify first. Expand only when expected value beats complexity and risk.
  10. Objective-checkDistinguish task completed from objective advanced.
  11. Reversal evidenceRecord what evidence would cause the decision to be reconsidered, and where it would appear.

6Builder protocol

The Builder may diagnose, design and implement inside approved scope. It may not self-certify acceptance, silently widen scope, or silently promote assumptions into facts.

ORF-6.1

Substantive work MUST open with the Builder header below.

ORF-6.2

The Builder MUST NOT remove existing features or controls unless removal is explicitly approved.

ORF-6.3

The Builder MUST NOT assert runtime behaviour on the basis of static reasoning (§7).

ORF-6.4

The Builder MUST NOT act on a scope expansion before a human decision is returned.

ORF-6.5

A material assumption MUST be labelled as assumed and MUST NOT be promoted to known or Verified without evidence. Where a material assumption can be checked at reasonable cost before it becomes implementation dependency, it SHOULD be checked.

ORF-6.6

For CHANGE and COMMITMENT work, the Builder SHOULD update one persistent Change Record rather than regenerate equivalent context as multiple standalone artifacts (§14).

Template — Builder header
Decision tier        : PATCH | CHANGE | COMMITMENT
Tier rationale       : impact / reversibility / uncertainty
Objective            : the actual outcome sought
Success criteria     : observable result
Governing spec       : path / link / identifier | none supplied
Constraints          : technical / security / commercial / time
Approved scope       : what may change
Out of scope         : what may not change
Material assumptions: assumptions that could change the decision
Floor proposal       : smallest viable change
Strongest alternative: credible competing approach
Template — Stop rule output
STOP RULE TRIGGERED
Scope expansion      : proposed addition or change
Why necessary        : evidence
Blast radius         : assessment
Bounded alternative  : if any
Recommended decision : proceed | alternative | defer
Status               : waiting for human decision

7Evidence states

Four words that are not interchangeable, and one rule that no amount of agreement can override.

StateMeaning
REVIEWEDImplementation was inspected statically.
TESTEDA defined check was executed.
VERIFIEDEvidence supports the specific material claim being made.
UNRESOLVEDAvailable evidence cannot settle the claim.

Where a change exists but required runtime or integration evidence was not obtained, the required words are: Implemented but not verified.

ORF-7.1 — Consensus fallacy rule

A runtime-required claim MUST NOT be closed by static review or by model agreement, however many models concur. For runtime-required behaviour the state is verified by runtime evidence or unresolved. There is no third state.

Runtime-required claims include

  • Deployment succeeds.
  • Concurrent writes are safe.
  • Authorization is enforced as intended.
  • Database migration behaves correctly.
  • Quota and rate-limit behaviour is correct.
  • Timezone and scheduled behaviour is correct under execution.
  • Failure recovery and retries behave correctly.
  • External integration works end to end.

8Auditor protocol and independence

The Auditor is a checker, not a builder. ORF audits the engineering method and project specification already in use; it does not replace them.

ORF-8.1

The Auditor MUST NOT modify the implementation, propose a redesign, or supply replacement code unless the decision owner explicitly changes its role.

ORF-8.2

Provenance MUST NOT be falsified. It SHOULD be withheld where practical: supply brief, constraints, diff, implementation and evidence without attribution.

ORF-8.3

Every audit MUST emit a Coverage Statement, including audits with zero findings (§9).

ORF-8.4

Each finding MUST cite the requirement or brief item violated, by identifier where one exists.

ORF-8.6

Where a governing project specification is supplied, the Auditor MUST treat its requirements, acceptance criteria and project-specific constraints as first-class audit requirements. ORF's generic risk checks are a baseline, not a ceiling.

ORF-8.7

If an expected governing specification, construction/constitution file, acceptance criteria or equivalent project artifact is unavailable, the Auditor MUST disclose that absence under EVIDENCE LIMITS in the Coverage Statement.

Compatibility with spec-driven development Added from public review

Governance overlay

In a spec-driven workflow, the project specification defines what is to be built; ORF governs how the AI Builder handles scope, evidence, verification and independent challenge. The Auditor contract inherits project-specific requirements from the governing specification instead of relying only on generic review prompts.

Degrees of independence

Independence is a spectrum, and a claim of independent audit MUST state which level was achieved. Proportionality remains tier-based: L3 is not mandatory for every PATCH.

LevelConditionWeight
L0Same session, same model, builder context retained.Self-review. Not an audit; must be labelled as such.
L1Fresh session, same model, context withheld.Detects regressions and brief deviations. Shares the builder's blind spots.
L2Different model family, context and attribution withheld.Minimum for CHANGE and above.
L3Different model family plus a human reviewer.Recommended for COMMITMENT and may be required by project policy.
ORF-8.5 — Correlated failure

Models trained on overlapping data can fail in overlapping directions. Agreement between AI reviewers MUST NOT be treated as independent corroboration and MUST NOT increase the verification state of a claim where correlated failure modes may exist. Disagreement SHOULD be treated as a signal requiring resolution through evidence or human decision, as applicable.

Template — Auditor contract
// Findings-only audit
Audit against the supplied brief, governing project specification,
acceptance criteria and constraints.
Do not rewrite or improve the implementation.

Project-specific requirements:
  inherit requirements / controls from the governing specification
  cite the project requirement identifier where one exists

Generic baseline checks:
  deviations from brief or constraints
  silent removals and regressions
  tier misclassification
  unsupported Verified claims
  unapproved scope expansion
  missing tests
  partial-failure, concurrency, auth, security,
    quota and rollback risks

Per finding:
  Severity | Finding | Evidence | Requirement violated | Verification required

9Coverage statement

Silence is not a clean audit. An audit that never says what it could not see is not itself auditable.

Template — Coverage statement
INDEPENDENCE LEVEL : L0 | L1 | L2 | L3
CHECKED            : areas actually inspected
NOT CHECKED        : areas not inspected, and why
RUNTIME REQUIRED   : claims that cannot be settled statically
EVIDENCE LIMITS    : missing brief, governing spec, diff, logs,
                       environment, credentials, test data
FINDINGS COUNT     : Critical n | High n | Medium n | Low n
AUDIT RESULT       : findings present
                     | no findings in checked scope
                     | inconclusive

Note the permitted results. No findings in checked scope is not clean, and inconclusive is a legitimate outcome that MUST NOT be upgraded for tidiness.

10Disagreement resolution

Disagreement exposes uncertainty. It is not settled by voting, and not by whichever explanation reads better.

DisagreementResolution
Behavioural, falsifiableDefine the smallest disputed claim and run a check capable of settling it.
Runtime-requiredRuntime evidence, or UNRESOLVED.
Maintainability, naming, boundary placement, long-term costLog as an OPEN QUESTION and route to the decision owner.
Insufficient evidenceUNRESOLVED until evidence becomes available.
ORF-10.1 — No rhetorical settlement

A material disagreement that no test can settle MUST NOT be marked resolved because one party argued more convincingly. It remains open, in writing, until the decision owner closes it.

11Re-entry and abort

Remediation is work, and work is classified.

ORF-11.1

Every remediation prompted by an audit finding MUST re-enter at CLASSIFY. A one-line fix in authentication middleware is not a PATCH because the finding was small.

ORF-11.2

Where a COMMITMENT fails after sign-off, the rollback or mitigation MUST itself be classified, and SHOULD be pre-classified at sign-off time rather than under incident pressure.New

ORF-11.3

Where the decision owner is unavailable, work MUST default to DEFER. Absence of a human response is never approval.New

12Evidence footer

Principles describe intent. A fixed output shape is the part that binds — a violated principle is invisible; a missing line is not.

Template — Evidence footer
Decision tier            : PATCH | CHANGE | COMMITMENT
Tier rationale           : impact / reversibility / uncertainty
Tier changes             : none | escalation history
Governing specification    : path / link / identifier | none supplied
Change Record              : identifier / path | compact PATCH record
Human downgrade          : none | recorded rationale
Assumed                  : material assumptions
Verified                 : claims + the evidence for each
Unverified / unresolved  : claims not settled
Scope added beyond brief : none | approved change
Verification run         : exact commands — PASS | FAIL | NOT RUN
Runtime-required claims  : verified by runtime evidence | unresolved
Original failure reproduced: yes | no | N/A
Regression checks        : what was checked
Remaining risks          : residual risks
Reversal evidence        : what would trigger reconsideration
Human sign-off required  : yes | no

For PATCH work the footer MAY be reduced to tier, verification run, unverified claims and scope added. Full footers on trivial work train people to stop reading them.

ORF-13.1

Records for CHANGE and COMMITMENT work — whether stored as one Change Record or linked artifacts — MUST retain the builder header, audit findings, coverage statement, evidence footer and sign-off where the next person to touch the system can find them. A record kept only in a chat transcript is not retained.New

ORF-13.2

Accepting an unresolved runtime claim is a permitted decision. Recording it as verified is not.

14Artifact economy and protocol healthExtended from public review

Governance must cost less than the failure it is meant to prevent. Produce the minimum evidence required by the tier, without hiding material uncertainty.

ORF-14.1

Protocol artifacts SHOULD be the minimum sufficient to satisfy the declared tier. Repeating unchanged context across artifacts is discouraged.

ORF-14.2

PATCH work SHOULD use the compact record permitted by this specification and MUST NOT require an independent audit solely for ORF conformance unless the task escalates or project policy requires one.

ORF-14.3

CHANGE and COMMITMENT work SHOULD maintain one evolving Change Record that references existing specifications, diffs, logs and test evidence rather than reproducing them in full.

ORF-14.4

Existing project artifacts SHOULD be referenced by stable path, link, commit, hash or identifier where that preserves auditability. Copying them into every ORF output is unnecessary.

ORF-14.5

Artifact economy MUST NOT be used to omit mandatory evidence, hide an unresolved material claim, or bypass a tier-required audit or sign-off.

ORF-14.6

For AI-executed workflows, context and token cost SHOULD be treated as engineering resources. Where the environment can retrieve a stable artifact by path, link, commit or identifier, unchanged content SHOULD be referenced rather than repeatedly reproduced.

Template — Evolving Change Record
Change ID            : stable identifier
Tier / rationale      : current tier + escalation history
Governing spec        : path / link / commit / identifier
Scope                 : approved / out of scope
Material assumptions  : current state + evidence links
Builder decisions     : concise decision log
Stop Rule events      : none | decisions and approvals
Verification evidence: commands / results / evidence refs
Audit                 : findings + coverage refs
Human decisions      : downgrade / risk acceptance / sign-off
Open questions       : unresolved material items

Cadence

  • At the start of a substantive session, sample one tier classification from the previous session.
  • Select one prior VERIFIED claim at random and inspect the evidence behind it.
  • Review recurring audit misses by strengthening tests, not by adding prose controls.
  • Do not run full ceremony on PATCH work unless the task escalates.

Health indicators

SignalReading
Escalation rate near zeroThe ratchet is decorative. Classification is being set once and defended.
PATCH share rising over timeCeremony may be being avoided by under-declaring. Sample harder.
Audits returning zero findings repeatedlyIndependence may be too weak in practice, or the audit package may be incomplete.
UNRESOLVED count always zeroRuntime claims may be getting closed statically somewhere.
Footers identical across tasksThe footer has become a template, not a report.
Artifact count grows without new evidenceThe protocol is generating ceremony rather than assurance. Consolidate into the Change Record.
Large context repeatedly copied between stagesToken and review cost are being spent on duplication. Reference stable project artifacts instead.

15ConformanceNew in this revision

A specification without a conformance clause cannot be complied with, only admired.

ORF-15.1

Conformance is claimed per change, never per team or per organisation. "We follow ORF-3.1" is not a conformance claim.

ORF-15.2

Conformant. Every MUST applicable to the declared tier was satisfied, and the record exists to show it.

ORF-15.3

Partially conformant. One or more MUST requirements were not met, each named with its identifier and the reason. This is an honest and permitted state.

ORF-15.4

Non-conformant. A MUST was not met and was not disclosed. Undisclosed shortfall is the only failure this specification treats as a breach of Rule 0.

16Versioning and change controlNew in this revision

ORF-16.1

The eleven principles are frozen. Additions are made to the governance layer, not the principle list.

Versioning applicability

The versioning rules in this section apply to releases following ORF-3.1. ORF-3.1 establishes the baseline governance requirements from which subsequent releases are versioned.

ORF-16.2

For releases after ORF-3.1, a change that adds or strengthens a MUST is a major version. A change that adds a SHOULD, a template or a clarification is a minor version.

ORF-16.3

Requirement identifiers are stable across versions. A withdrawn requirement is marked withdrawn; its identifier is never reused.

ORF-16.4

Tier thresholds SHOULD be refined from observed misclassification in real work, not from theoretical edge cases.
